# Abdul-Ghaffur Jimoh — Portfolio

Personal portfolio featuring an ecommerce sales and transaction-review case study.
It also includes a product catalogue quality review using 100 real Open Food Facts records, and a synthetic supplier-catalogue cleanup that turns three supplier files into one import-ready master.

## Website

GitHub Pages address: https://abdulghaffurjimoh.github.io/

## Included work

- A sales analysis of all 541,909 UCI Online Retail source records.
- An Excel dashboard, product and customer analysis, and documented data-quality checks.
- A review of the 25 largest credit lines with source references, possible sale matches and next steps.
- A catalogue handover review: 52 records needing verification, 48 passing selected checks and 35 traceable field changes.
- A supplier-catalogue cleanup: 268 raw supplier rows reduced to 250 unique SKUs, with 18 duplicates merged, 12 SKU formats and 9 price formats handled, 210 records ready, 37 held for a supplier answer and Shopify, BigCommerce and generic import files.

The supplier-catalogue case study is a synthetic sample, clearly labelled as such. It is not client work, and its product names, prices, images and barcodes are fictional. It demonstrates cleaning rules, traceability and import-file structure, not commercial results. Full evidence, including raw files, validation results and a hash manifest, is in `supplier-catalogue-evidence.zip`.

The catalogue case study is a public-data exercise. Open Food Facts data and derived extracts are offered under ODbL 1.0, with individual contents under DbCL 1.0. Full source details, scope, rules and attribution are included in `catalogue-evidence.zip`. Passing selected checks does not approve a store import.

This is a public-data portfolio project, not a commissioned client engagement. The source is Chen, D. (2015), Online Retail, UCI Machine Learning Repository, https://doi.org/10.24432/C5BW33, CC BY 4.0. Source attribution and limitations are on the case-study page and in the workbooks.

The static site uses HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Fonts are Instrument Serif and DM Sans through Google Fonts. No backend or analytics is required.
