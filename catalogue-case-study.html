What the checks found

Pack information
needs attention first.

38 records have no pack size. Another seven contain a multipack, a count-only label or an ambiguous value that the preparation rules do not convert.

Missing categories affect 32 records. A category supplied in the source is retained as source text; it still needs mapping to the destination store’s taxonomy.

Issue counts overlap. One product can appear in several categories. These figures describe this sample, not the whole database.

Loading the product records…

Clear rules.
Traceable changes.

A useful handover should let someone retrace the work without guessing how the data changed.

Preserve the source.

The original JSON and selected source fields are retained. Every output record has its original row number, product code and source URL.

Make limited corrections.

Unicode and whitespace are normalised. Explicit single-pack mass and volume labels are standardised. 34 pack labels and one product name changed format, and 55 quantities could be expressed in grams or millilitres.

Leave uncertainty visible.

Missing fields and ambiguous pack sizes enter a review queue with a next action. Supported GTIN check digits are tested; all 100 codes pass, which confirms their checksum only.

Prepare the handover.

The workbook includes an editable follow-up status and evidence notes. The CSV, issue queue and change log remain separate so a team can work through corrections and check what changed.
